What it handles

Authentication, every way

Email and password, one-time codes by email or SMS, Telegram, and social sign-in with Google, GitHub, Microsoft, Apple and Facebook. Enable only what an app needs.

A real OpenID Connect provider

Authorization code flow with PKCE, refresh tokens, discovery and JWKS. Anything that speaks OIDC — Supabase, Grafana, Metabase — integrates with no custom code.

Single sign-on that is actually single

Group your applications into one directory and a user signs in once. The second app gets a token without ever showing a login form.

Tenant isolation by default

Each tenant has its own user directory, branding, roles and database schema. A lookup in one tenant cannot return another tenant's user — enforced in the database, not just the code.

Payments on local rails

ABA PayWay for Cambodia, Gumroad internationally, and a manual receipt queue with an approval SLA. Merchant credentials belong to each tenant, so revenue lands in their account.

Secrets and configuration

Encrypted values your app reads at runtime through the SDK, with a local cache so a Bifrost outage does not take your app down with it.

A console operators can use

User and role management, a content editor over the tenant's own database, the payment queue, and an audit log with before-and-after values on every change.

Roles that mean something

Per-application roles resolved into permission claims inside the token, so your app authorises without a second lookup.

Least privilege, including ours

Platform staff see configuration, metrics and the audit log for external tenants — not their secrets, content, users or payments. Deeper access is granted by the tenant, and revoked by them.

Integrating

  1. 1
    Register your application

    Get a client_id and client_secret, and set your callback URL.

  2. 2
    Send users to Bifrost

    Redirect to the hosted sign-in page, or drive the OpenID Connect endpoints directly.

  3. 3
    Receive a signed token

    Verify it against the published JWKS. It carries the user, their role and their permissions.
